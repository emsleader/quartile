2. Research Knowledge Base

Claude reads the full text and records the contribution, hypotheses, constructs, variable relationships, findings and limitations, quoting the manuscript. The deterministic layer records every number, citation, hedge and causal phrase. Every edit is checked against this record.

3. Verified Q1 library

Searches Elicit's index with a journal-quartile filter of Q1, removes duplicates by DOI, then Claude assigns Tier A, B or C and a relevance score from the abstracts. Elicit reports the quartile at journal level; the subject category and ranking year still need checking in SCImago, so each row links there.

Journal finder: Q1 to Q4

Searches Elicit for papers closest to your manuscript and ranks the journals that publish them, separately for each quartile, so you can choose between the most prestigious fit and an easier entry.

Journal bank

Every business, management, accounting, economics, finance and decision-sciences journal in SCImago Journal Rank 2024 ( journals). Difficulty is a 0 to 100 index built from SCImago figures: SJR percentile within the journal's main category (45%), H-index (20%), citations per paper (20%) and how few papers it publishes (15%). It measures prestige and competition, not acceptance rates, which SCImago does not publish; and an accessible journal can still desk-reject a paper that does not fit its scope.

References

Matches every in-text citation to the reference list, then looks each reference up in Elicit's index to confirm it exists and that its year and DOI agree. "Not found" means Elicit could not match it, not that it is fake; check those by hand.

4. Academic writing profile

Claude studies the abstracts of the top-ranked library articles and describes general conventions: sentence and paragraph construction, hedging, gap framing, reporting verbs. It records patterns only and does not copy source sentences. Abstracts are used because full texts are not always licensed for analysis.

Desk-rejection screen

Instant, rule-based and without AI. Checks the five reasons editors most often give for desk rejection: no original or substantive contribution, weak structure, weak writing, thin grounding in the literature, and poor fit to the journal. Paste the journal's guidelines in step 6 to check its formal requirements as well.

Integrity screen (COPE-aligned)

Checks your manuscript for reused wording before submission, following the logic of the COPE flowchart Plagiarism in a submitted manuscript. Add your own earlier papers (overlap there is redundancy, which needs citation and disclosure) and any sources you worked from. Each sentence is compared for runs of 8 or more identical words; overlap is graded as none, minor or substantial, with what an editor following COPE would do and what to fix. Files are read in this page only. This is not a web-wide plagiarism check.

Measures your abstract (length, sentence length, active voice, hedging, causal verbs, stock words, numbers, sample-size clause, rhetorical moves) and compares it with Quartile's measured profile of about 500 recent, highly cited Q1 abstracts from 76 journals, by field or by journal. 5. Q1 review

A skeptical reviewer report against the selected writing type, the 14 rules and your knowledge base. It scores rule compliance and lists major and minor issues with quotes from the text. It is not a prediction of acceptance.

Idea → Q1 paper frame

Give a raw research idea. The engine frames it the way papers at the chosen tier frame theirs: a puzzle or contradiction in named prior work, one central claim, a theory lens, testable hypotheses, an identification strategy, rival explanations to rule out, boundary conditions and the contribution stated as a change to existing theory. When the Elicit connector is available it first retrieves real Q1 papers on the idea, and only those papers may be cited. Anything else is marked [citation needed]. No results or numbers are invented: expected findings are written as predictions to test.

Statistics and regression check

Exact recomputation without AI, in the spirit of statcheck. It recomputes p from reported t(df), F(df1, df2), r(df), χ²(df) and z, allowing for rounding, and flags decision errors that cross .05. It checks that coefficient ÷ SE matches the reported t and p, that significance stars match the coefficient and SE under the 10/5/1% or 5/1/0.1% convention, and that R², adjusted R², alpha, AVE, r and p lie in possible ranges. It also recognizes the output format of Stata, R, SPSS, EViews, Python statsmodels, SmartPLS and AMOS/Mplus, tests whether the last digits of the reported decimals are uniform, and flags p-values clustered just below .05.

What it cannot do: no test can prove that a number came from R, Stata, a person or an AI. It can show that the numbers do not fit together, or that they look unlike genuine software output, which is exactly what a reviewer or data editor will check. To prove origin, keep the script and log file (an R script, a Stata do-file and log) and share them as replication material.

Runs on the whole manuscript, tables included.

Issues: review first, then apply

Every problem found in the manuscript is listed here before anything changes: grammar, AI-style wording, clarity, claim strength, causal wording, terminology, Q1 rules, references and desk-rejection risks. For each one you can apply the fix, ignore it, or write a comment telling the engine exactly what to do (for example "only change this word" or "keep my phrasing, just fix the tense"). Fixes that replace an exact phrase are applied directly, without AI. Every applied change passes the hallucination guard.

8. Validate and approve

Finish automatically repairs every flagged paragraph (up to two rounds), re-audits it, accepts everything that passes all checks and keeps your original wherever a problem remains. Each revised paragraph is checked twice: by exact rules (numbers, citations, protected terms, hedges, causal wording, negations) and by a separate Claude audit that compares meaning, argument strength and variable relationships. Nothing enters the final text until you accept it.

9. Export

The final manuscript uses accepted revisions and keeps your original wording everywhere else.
