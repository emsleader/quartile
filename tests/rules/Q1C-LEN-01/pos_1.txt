# Abstract

We study how small service firms in nine European countries finance the replacement of expensive equipment when bank credit is scarce. Our data come from a survey of owners and managers collected between 2016 and 2021. We link the survey to firm accounts filed with national registers in each of the nine countries. Firms that report a rejected loan application delay the replacement of their main machines by about fourteen months on average. The delay is longest for young firms, for firms without real estate collateral and for firms in rural regions. The association remains after we control for sector, country, year, firm size, owner experience and prior investment. It is similar when we use an alternative measure of credit constraints based on discouraged borrowers. Discouraged borrowers are firms that did not apply because they expected a refusal from their bank. We also show that leasing and supplier credit partly replace bank loans for firms with strong customer demand. These substitutes are expensive and short, so they do not remove the gap between constrained and unconstrained firms. The gap persists over a five-year horizon in all nine countries and in both manufacturing-related and personal services. We discuss why lenders and public guarantee schemes that look only at collateral may miss viable service firms. The main asset of many such firms is a stream of repeat customers rather than buildings or land. We describe simple changes in loan assessment that could reduce delays in equipment replacement for this group. We close with the limits of survey data on loan applications and with the records that a later study would need. Those records include the date of each application, the amount requested and the reason for any refusal given by the bank. A register of guarantee claims would also show which refused firms later received public support. Such a register exists in several of the nine countries but is not yet open to researchers.
