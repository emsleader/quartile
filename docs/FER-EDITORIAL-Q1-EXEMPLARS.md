### Published full-text example actually inspected: A1

The publisher HTML for A1 was directly read. Main headings are: 1 Introduction; 2 Institutional framework; 3 Literature review and hypothesis development; 4 Data and empirical methodology; 5 Results (including subgroup and robustness checks); 6 Discussion and conclusions; appendices.

In that directly inspected HTML, an approximate heuristic gives **12 body paragraphs** in the introduction, **18** in data/methodology and **6** in discussion/conclusions; equations and embedded figure captions make the methods count approximate. This is one article's structure, not a 100-paper writing benchmark.

The opening moves in the Introduction are: economic crisis/institutional context → monetary-policy response → research question → SME relevance → claimed literature contribution → monetary-transmission discussion → heterogeneity. The Methods moves are: survey source and coverage → variables and outcome construction → measurement decisions → policy exposure → explicit regression equation and interpretation. The Discussion moves are: restate the estimand and main outcomes → heterogeneity and null findings → concrete but conditional policy implications → limitations and future research.

**Use these as rhetorical functions, never as text to copy or as a mandate to force the FER article into identical paragraph counts.**

### Documented selection constraint: World Bank Enterprise Surveys

The World Bank catalog for the Hungary 2009–2013–2019–2023 panel notes that the provided wave weights are representative of the universe **in the interview year, and are not panel weights** (https://microdata.worldbank.org/catalog/8141/study-description). The WBES methodology explains changes in eligible sector coverage from August 2022 and adjustment of panel inclusion probabilities (https://www.enterprisesurveys.org/en/methodology). This is directly relevant to the FER manuscript's central distinction between repeated cross-section and reinterviewed establishments.

## One-paragraph-at-a-time manuscript workflow

1. Freeze the registered paper's results, definitions, claims, citations and analysis files.
2. For each FER editor requirement, read the most appropriate *published full-text* article from A/B, making a structured note with DOI, source access, equation/section placement, rhetorical moves, and policy inference limits.
3. Create a section outline for FER, with one rhetorical purpose per paragraph; map both incoming and outgoing transitions.
4. Draft or edit **one paragraph only**, using confirmed manuscript evidence and the preceding locked paragraph(s).
5. Run Quartile's paragraph-flow diagnostics and integrity guard; adjudicate semantic coherence and numeric/citation accuracy by scientific review.
6. Approve and lock the paragraph before drafting the next; inspect a whole section for progression only after all paragraphs are individually approved.
7. Distinguish stylistic revision from new econometric analysis. New estimates require source data, executable code, diagnostic checks and the author's approval.
